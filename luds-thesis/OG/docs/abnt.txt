Who we are

ABNT is the National Standardization Forum for recognition of Brazilian society since its foundation, on September 28, 1940, and confirmed by the federal government through various legal instruments.

A private and non-profit entity, ABNT is a founding member of the International Organization for Standardization (International Organization for Standardization – ISO), the Pan American Commission for Technical Standards (Pan American Commission for Technical Standards – Copant) and the Mercosur Association for Standardization (Mercosur Association for Standardization – AMN). Since its foundation, it is also a member of the International Electrotechnical Commission (International Electrotechnical Commission – IEC).

ABNT is responsible for the elaboration of the Brazilian Standards (ABNT NBR), prepared by its Brazilian Committees (ABNT/CB), Sectoral Standardization Bodies (ABNT/ONS) and Special Study Commissions (ABNT/EEC).

Since 1950, ABNT also acts in conformity assessment and has programs for certification of products, systems and environmental labeling. This activity is based on internationally accepted guides and technical principles and based on a technical structure and multidisciplinary auditors, ensuring credibility, ethics and recognition of the services provided.

Working in harmony with governments and society, ABNT contributes to the implementation of public policies, promotes the development of markets, consumer protection and the security of all citizens.

How can an object be produced?

How do we know how to do something, whether it's an axe or a silex knife, an airplane or a fabric, a brick or a circuit to a computer? Whether for personal use or for an economic exchange, it is not enough, to begin with, that only a man can produce such objects. Nor is it any use that he is able to do it only once. The product of human labor is the product of human society, and knowing how to do something means that many men, now and in the future, anywhere, at any time, also need to be able to do.

In reality, theoretical or practical knowledge, devoid of the means for its conservation and transmission, means little in itself. Human work becomes material through procedures, rules, instructions, models, which can be repeated, taught and learned. Without this fundamental condition – the expression of knowledge in rules understandable by the other – the material civilization is unable to reproduce. Teaching and learning to create are acts that require a common language.
